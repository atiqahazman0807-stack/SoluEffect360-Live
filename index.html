Live Explanation

Water: only water molecules are present, so only water molecules can enter the vapour phase.

Non-volatile solute: the solute remains in the liquid; only the solvent contributes to vapour pressure.

Volatile solute: both the solvent and volatile solute can enter the vapour phase, so both partial pressures contribute to the total vapour pressure.

Experiment Summary

Observation

A non-volatile solute lowers the solvent vapour pressure. A volatile solute contributes its own vapour, so the total vapour pressure can be higher than in the non-volatile case.

Real-life Application

A wet towel soaked in salty water can dry more slowly because dissolved salt lowers the mole fraction and vapour pressure of the solvent, reducing its tendency to evaporate.

Live Explanation

A liquid boils when its vapour pressure becomes equal to the external pressure.

A non-volatile solute lowers the solvent vapour pressure, so a higher temperature is needed.

The non-volatile solute remains in the liquid phase. As a result, the solution reaches the external pressure only at a temperature above the boiling point of the pure solvent.

Experiment Summary

Observation

A solution containing a non-volatile solute boils at a higher temperature than the pure solvent because the solute lowers the solvent vapour pressure.

Real-life Application

Special liquids (coolants) are added to car water tanks. This raises the boiling point so the water inside the hot engine doesn't boil away easily.

Live Explanation

Freezing occurs when the liquid and solid phases of a solvent are in equilibrium.

Dissolved solute particles lower the escaping tendency of solvent molecules in the liquid phase.

Therefore, the solution must be cooled below the freezing point of the pure solvent before the solid solvent can form. This decrease is called freezing-point depression.

Experiment Summary

Observation

Pure water freezes exactly at 0 °C and forms a neat grid. The mixture stays liquid at 0 °C and only freezes when it gets even colder.

Real-life Application

Antifreeze is added to vehicle cooling systems in cold climates so the coolant remains liquid at temperatures where the pure solvent could freeze.

Live Explanation

Osmosis is the net movement of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane from a region of lower solute concentration to a region of higher solute concentration.

The membrane allows solvent molecules to pass but prevents solute particles from crossing.

The net movement continues until the concentration difference is balanced by the pressure difference across the membrane.

Experiment Summary

Observation

Water molecules move in both directions. The net movement causes water to accumulate on the solution side until the pressure difference balances the osmotic tendency.

Real-life Application

Plant roots soak up water from the soil using osmosis. If you put too much fertilizer (solid particles) in the soil, the plant cannot pull water in, and might die.
